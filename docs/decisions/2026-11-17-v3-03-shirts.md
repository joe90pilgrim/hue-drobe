# V3-03: Shirts, long, mid and short sleeves

Date: 17 Nov 2026. Status: built and passing the hard checks. Joe rejected the first pass, and a second pass followed the same day (see "Second pass"). **Not yet signed off by Joe or Beth.**

## Decisions (Joe, 17 Nov 2026)
- Collar: a classic point collar. Tucked or untucked: untucked first (so, like the sweatshirt, a shirt cannot be worn with the skirt; V3-05 will decide tucking).

## What was built
- `garment.add_shirt_details` puts a point collar (stand, fold, flap with two points) and a raised placket on the tee's offset shell. Shape only, no buttons.
- Three meshes per body: `shirt_short`, `shirt_mid`, `shirt_long`. Chest ease and length are the tee's; short is the tee's sleeve; mid ends just above the elbow; long ends 1 cm short of the wrist joint. Sizes in `pack/fit_config.json` `shirt`.
- Held to the tee's hard mesh limits, calibration and bust-shape limits, and checked against trousers, shorts and tights, plus two collar points and a raised placket (`spike/tolerances.json` `shirt`, `check_reports.py --shirts`).
- App: three options in the Top slot ("Shirt, short/mid/long sleeves"), each its own garment id, so saved items and outfits need **no new field** (the data model is unchanged). Each length keeps its own colour. Offered only when the pack has the mesh, so an older pack still opens.

## Numbers (all nine bodies: six synthetic, Joe, B; `pack/reports` refreshed)
All hard checks pass for all three lengths: 0 body-through-cloth pairs, 0 self-intersections, minimum clearance 0.3 cm, 0 pairs with trousers, shorts and tights, shoulder gap within the tee's limits, chest within 0.0 cm and length within 0.0 cm of target, sleeve and cuff within 0.9 cm. Joe's pack is 1324 KB and B's 2400 KB (limit 15 MB). Phone frame rate not measured yet.

## Things that did not work, and what was changed
- Neckline narrower than the tee's (0.10 and 0.11 of height): broke the shell (chest unreadable on the synthetic man; B's hips thrown out). Cause not found; the shirt uses the tee's width.
- A longer tail (0.41 of height) was unstable on B; the shirt uses the tee's length (0.39).
- The mid sleeve is capped at 93% of the upper arm on a body where the config value is longer.
- A bolder collar (3 cm stand) and placket (0.8 cm) made shirts clip the trousers on some bodies; reverted.

## Needs Joe's agreement (spike/tolerances.json)
- `shirt.shoulder_gap_from_x_cm` = 10: the shirt's shoulder gap is judged from 10 cm out, not 4 cm, because the collar lies over the base of the neck (with the collar in the band the mean was 0.57 cm against 0.5; outside it the shoulders measure the same as the tee's).
- `shirt.collar_tip_drop_cm_min` 4.0, `placket_height_cm_min` 0.3, `placket_vertices_min` 20 are my proposals.

## Second pass (17 Nov 2026, Opus, after Joe rejected the first)
Joe's complaints and what each turned out to be:
1. **"B's body pokes through the shirt."** No skin is drawn through it: an app-side depth test (render the shirt alone, then with the body; count pixels where skin wins within 2 cm of the shirt's outside) found 0 pixels for all three shirts over trousers, shorts, shorts with tights, tights alone and nothing, at 72 camera angles each. The same test found 9,800 to 22,500 pixels per view when the shirt was shrunk by 5 mm, so it does find pokes. Joe confirmed what he meant: the shirt showed B's nipples, ribs and navel like "a skin-tight wrap", not cotton. Cause: not shape (at the navel the shirt is 2 to 3 cm off the skin) but shading. The shell inherits the body template's vertex layout, dense at the navel and nipples (5 mm edges against 20 to 30 mm elsewhere), and its millimetre ripples there, shaded with per-vertex normals, draw the anatomy on the cloth. A hollow-filling pass that lifts the cloth outward did not change the look and was dropped.
   - Fix: `garment.set_shading_normals` writes the plain cloth's normals averaged over 5 cm (custom normals, exported as NORMAL); the shape is unchanged, so no hard check moves.
   - New check, `mesh_checks.shading_look` on each exported GLB: over the plain torso cloth, the largest turn of the shading normal within 1.5 cm along the cloth. Shirts are held to `shirt.shading_kink_deg_max` 22 (`check_reports.py`). The first shirts measured 40.5 on B, so this check would have caught it.
2. **Colour picker did nothing on shirts.** The ce3c239 fix holds: in the app, B's short shirt changes to #2E5E8C, #1F2A44 and #F2F2F2.
3. **Collar invisible.** Now drawn so it reads in the app's light in any plain colour:
   - the collar's edge turns back towards the cloth (a 0.45 cm lip), so it has a visible outline;
   - the fold lines are sharp edges;
   - the flap stands further off the cloth (0.8 cm fold, 0.7 cm gap);
   - the points are corners (straight drop to each point, steep rise after it, a 0.8 cm notch at the front centre);
   - drawn shading in the file (glTF COLOR_0, multiplied by the colour picked in the app) darkens the lip to 0.45, the placket edges to 0.55 and the band to 0.8, with a soft shadow on the cloth within 1.5 cm of the collar's edge.
   
   The placket is now cut into the shell along straight lines, raised 0.4 cm straight forward with 0.3 cm edge slopes (sharp edges, darker). Raising it along the vertex normal had pointed it the wrong way at the tail. Exported by name (`export_vertex_color="NAME"`): "ACTIVE" wrote a blank attribute as COLOR_0 and the shading as COLOR_1, which three.js ignores. A new check fails a shirt file whose COLOR_0 is missing or never darker than `shirt.detail_colour_min_max` 0.6.
   - The shirt's chest is now calibrated on the shell before the collar and placket go on. The placket's edges added about 0.8 cm round the chest, which took 0.06 cm of ease out of the shirt, and the trousers' waistband then crossed the synthetic man's shirt (118 to 140 face pairs) until this was changed.
