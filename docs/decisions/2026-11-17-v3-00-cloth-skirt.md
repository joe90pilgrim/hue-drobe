# V3-00 Experiment: an A-line midi skirt with Blender cloth

Date: 17 November 2026. Result: **the skirt passes every hard mesh check on B (0 body pairs, 0 self pairs, 0.62 cm clearance) and looks like a real A-line midi. It fails on both synthetic bodies, for two different reasons.** One is a fault in how the start shape is built and is easy to fix. The other is not understood yet. Two builds of the same body are identical to the vertex. The pack's long skirt, the baseline to beat, had 103 to 577 body pairs. Recommendation: **go, with conditions** (below). Joe decides.

Skirt shape: A-line midi, chosen by Joe on 16 November 2026. Code: `spike/cloth/` (README there). Blender 5.3.0, MPFB 2.0.18, Blender's own cloth only. Reports, GLBs and renders are in `private/cloth_spike/` on Joe's machine, git-ignored because B's renders show a real body.

## What was run

- The body and the app's tee come from the unchanged `body_from_measurements.py` (B from `private/measurements_b.json`; synthetic female small and large from `spike/fixtures/`).
- **Start shape**, made from the body GLB: rings every 2 cm, 96 around, from the waist to mid-calf (21% of height above the floor; the body report has no knee or ankle). From the waist to the hip it follows the body's outline (convex hull of each 2 cm slice, arms left out) plus 1 cm of ease at the waist, rising to 2.5 cm at the hip. Below the hip it flares linearly to 1.5 times the skirt's hip circumference and turns from the hip's shape into a circle. The start shape is also the cloth's rest shape.
- **Cloth:** Blender's cotton preset with bending raised to 5 (0.5 crumpled the hem), quality 10, 80 frames. The top two rings are pinned, the body is the collider (friction 5) and self-collision is on. One Blender thread.
- **Checks:** `mesh_checks.py` on the exported GLB read back in, against the limits in `spike/tolerances.json` (0 body pairs, 0 self pairs, clearance at least 0.2 cm, one piece, two openings).

## What I saw

- **B:** a clean A-line midi with soft, even flare and no visible clipping from any side. The hem sits 2 cm below the target.
- **Large:** the same clean A-line from below the hip. Two faults: the waistband's top edge dips into the belly at the front, and there is a small step at each side near the hip, where the fitted part meets the flare.
- **Small:** not acceptable. The hem turns up and under at the knees into a bubble, 16 cm above the target.
- **With the tee:** the tee hangs over the skirt's waist and the two cut through each other everywhere they overlap (658 to 1008 pairs). Wearing the two together needs a layering rule (the tee tucked in, or the skirt waist made to sit outside the tee), which the app does not have.

## Why each failure happened

1. **Large, waistband edge: a start-shape fault, not the cloth.** 28 of the 30 pairs are already in the start shape. The top ring takes its outline from a 2 cm slice at the waist, and on this body the belly bulges just above that, so the edge dips in. Fix: take the outline over a few centimetres above the top ring as well. Not done, because the ceiling was reached.
2. **Small, hem riding up: not understood.** The start shape is clear of the body (0 pairs, 0.78 cm). The same body with bending 0.5 kept the hem at 32 cm but crumpled it. Leg friction is not the cause (friction 1 gave 48.7 cm), and neither is mass (0.1 gave 49.1 cm). My best guess, untested, is that the splayed A-pose legs catch a stiff, narrow hem (153 cm round) as it swings in and push it up. The things to try next are the leg stance, more flare on narrow bodies, or settling with gravity raised in steps.

## Recommendation

**Go, with conditions.** On the body that matters most, B, the technique gives the first loose garment to pass our hard checks. Builds are repeatable to the vertex on one thread, cost about a minute of cloth per body, and weigh about 110 KB. That is far better than the pack skirt. But a method that has passed on one body in three is not proven. V3-01 should start only on these terms:

- Fix the waistband outline (cause known), and get the small body to pass before any second skirt style. If the small body still fails after about 2 hours, stop and report.
- Every skirt is built and checked per body at asset time, as here. A blended in-between size has not been tested and should not be assumed to work.
- Agree a layering rule for tops with skirts before showing them together.

## What was not done or not reliable

- **Ceiling.** About 1 hour 45 minutes of working time. The computer slept for about 7 hours during one batch of runs (those builds report about 25,800 s for the body). Those runs were variant tests only; the results above come from a clean final run.
- **No phone test.** Frame rate and load on Joe's phone were not measured. At about 110 KB and 6,000 triangles per skirt, they should be similar to the tee.
- **Joe's body not run.** The ticket scope is B and two synthetic female bodies.
- **The hip found is not the tape hip.** The pattern's hip is the widest outline in the upper three quarters of waist to crotch. On the small body that outline is 85.3 cm, against a 90 cm tape hip. It sets where the flare starts, not the body.
- **Mid-calf is a fixed fraction of height** (21%), not a measured landmark.
- **No real skirt to compare against.** Hem and flare are a pattern chosen by me, not measured from a garment of Beth's.
- **Settings were tuned on two bodies in three test rounds** (bending 0.5 to 5, hem ratio 1.6 to 1.5, hip search, friction and mass on the small body). They were not tuned on the large body.
- **Three Blender builds in parallel stalled while the computer slept.** Two of them recovered on waking. The third was stopped.
